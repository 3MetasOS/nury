5. The technical sell, with proof: Gloo AI Studio guarded endpoint, privacy (tokens, not names), Jev typed judges plus a red team, a person reviews. VERIFIED claims only (documents/TECH_CLAIMS.md). Disclosure caption verbatim.
6. The memorial: Nury Pelaez, last thing on screen. Juan's words, text only.
Hard limits: at most 90 s; no real people, no real children, no agency imagery or names; no stock photos of detainees; humanitarian, never political; Eric is the narrator (ElevenLabs); the memorial has no synthetic voice.

## 2. Craft principles to apply (research and cite; do not just trust this list)
- **Hook in 3 seconds**; the first frame is a question or an image, not a logo.
- **One protagonist, one want, one clock.** Persona first: name, age, the room, the object in his hand. The viewer must care before the product appears. Tension comes from time pressure and consequence, not from music alone.
- **Problem, agitate, solve** (PAS), or the story spine (once upon a time, every day, until one day, because of that, until finally). Pick one; map each beat to seconds.
- **Show, don't tell.** The product proves itself in action. Narration says less than the picture shows. Narration lines of 8 words or fewer; silence is a tool.
- **Sound is half the film.** Room tone, a phone buzz, a clock tick that slows, a held breath, then a warm low pad when the lantern lights. Design the audio arc: sparse, tense, release. Make or license every sound (no unlicensed music); a synthesized pad and foley made in code are fine.
- **Contrast and arc of light.** Dark to light is our idea: the story starts in night (ink), the lantern lights (amber), the film ends in dawn (paper). That also answers Juan's note that the all-dark look is heavy.
- **Pace**: cut on action, hold on faces and hands (silhouettes and hands only; no identifiable people), one idea per shot, 2 to 4 s per shot, a held beat before the turn, a breath before the memorial.
- **Typography as a character**: large, calm, few words on screen; captions for muted viewing.
- **End on a feeling, then the name.** Memorial last.
- Sources to consult and cite (read pages only): hackathon and Demo Day video guidance (for example Y Combinator and Devpost judging advice), Pixar's story spine, PAS copywriting, sound design for short films, and the Remotion docs for cinematic motion (camera moves, light, type).
