# Standards alignment: Nury as case management

Written 2026-10-07 by hack-ninja for hack-sensei and Juan. Research from pages I read (raw text, not summaries, unless a row says otherwise). Evidence for what Nury does comes from `documents/FEATURES.md` and the code.

## Wording rules (read first)
- Say **"informed by"** or **"aligned with"**. Never "compliant with", "certified", "meets" or "follows" a standard.
- Nury is a **drafting aid for a pastor**. It is not a clinical or professional case-management system, and the pastor is not acting as a licensed case manager because of it.
- **No body endorses Nury.** None of the organizations below has reviewed, approved or endorsed it.
- Nury gives legal information and drafts. The pastor decides. Nury does not give advice, advocate, or predict.

### 1a. NASW Standards for Social Work Case Management (2013)
Source: NASW, *NASW Standards for Social Work Case Management*, 2013, (c) National Association of Social Workers. I read the full text (about 11,000 words) from the copy at https://ovcsupport.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/standards-case-management.pdf (U.S. Office for Victims of Crime training and technical assistance site). The NASW page is https://www.socialworkers.org/Practice/NASW-Practice-Standards-Guidelines/NASW-Standards-for-Social-Work-Case-Management. I did not read that page: the one mirror I tried returned HTTP 403. Status of every row in this table: **VERIFIED** (exact words, from the 2013 PDF).

| # | Standard | The statement, verbatim |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ethics and Values | "The social work case manager shall adhere to and promote the ethics and values of the social work profession, using the NASW Code of Ethics as a guide to ethical decision making in case management practice." |
| 2 | Qualifications | Requires a social work degree from an accredited program and compliance with "the licensing and certification requirements of the state(s) or jurisdiction(s)". |
| 3 | Knowledge | "...shall acquire and maintain knowledge of current theory, evidence-informed practice, sociohistorical context, policy, research, and evaluation methods relevant to case management and the population served..." |
| 4 | Cultural and Linguistic Competence | "...shall provide and facilitate access to culturally and linguistically appropriate services..." |
| 5 | Assessment | "...shall engage clients... in an ongoing information-gathering and decision-making process to help clients identify their goals, strengths, and challenges." |
| 6 | Service Planning, Implementation, and Monitoring | "...shall collaborate with clients to plan, implement, monitor, and amend individualized services that promote clients' strengths, advance clients' well-being, and help clients achieve their goals. Case management service plans shall be based on meaningful assessments and shall have specific, attainable, measurable objectives." |
| 7 | Advocacy and Leadership | "...shall advocate for the rights, decisions, strengths, and needs of clients and shall promote clients' access to resources, supports, and services." |
| 8 | Interdisciplinary and Interorganizational Collaboration | "...shall promote collaboration among colleagues and organizations to enhance service delivery and facilitate client goal attainment." |
| 9 | Practice Evaluation and Improvement | "...shall participate in ongoing, formal evaluation of her or his practice to advance client well-being, assess the appropriateness and effectiveness of services and supports, ensure competence, and improve practice." |

Social work documentation shall be recorded on paper or electronically and shall be prepared, completed, secured, maintained, and disclosed in accordance with regulatory, legislative, statutory, and organizational requirements." |
| 11 | Workload Sustainability | About advocating for "a caseload and scope of work that permit high-quality planning, provision, and evaluation". |
| 12 | Professional Development and Competence | About personal responsibility for professional development and competence. |

More from the same document (VERIFIED):
- The six core functions: "engagement with clients", "assessment of client priorities, strengths, and challenges", "development and implementation of a care plan", "monitoring of service delivery", "evaluation of outcomes" and "closure (including termination or transition follow-up)".
- On confidentiality: "The social work case manager must hold all client information in confidence. Such information may be released to other members of the family system, other service providers or organizations, or other parties only with written permission of the client (or the individual legally authorized to represent the client)." And: "certain limits of confidentiality are inherent in service delivery."
- On consent: "documentation should reflect clients' informed consent regarding all aspects of case management practice: assessment; service planning, delivery, and monitoring; practice evaluation and improvement; and transfer or termination of services."
- On who counts as the client: "The client system includes both the client and members of the client's support network (such as family members, friends, religious communities, or service providers)."

### 1c. Psychological First Aid (PFA)

VERIFIED from the page: "humane, supportive and practical help to fellow human beings suffering serious crisis events" and "a framework for supporting people in ways that respect their dignity, culture and abilities." **NOT VERIFIED from WHO:** the words "look, listen and link". VERIFIED from the page: "This evidence-informed approach helps to assist children, adolescents, adults, and families in the aftermath of disaster and terrorism." The page says the guide covers "the eight core actions" but does not list them.
- Look, Listen, Link and the eight core actions: **SECONDARY** (Wikipedia, "Psychological first aid", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_first_aid, read as raw text). It says the model is "commonly described using the three-step model of Look, Listen, and Link" with: Look: "Observe the situation and identify individuals who may require immediate support, protection, or assistance." Listen: "Approach people respectfully, assess their needs and concerns, and listen without pressure or judgement." Link: "Help people address immediate needs, access information, connect with loved ones, and obtain additional services where required." The eight core actions it lists: contact and engagement; safety and comfort; stabilization; information gathering; practical assistance; connection with social supports; information on coping; linkage with collaborative services.
- A limit to say out loud: the same Wikipedia article says "While it is based on research, it is not proven by research." (SECONDARY.)

### 1d. SAMHSA trauma-informed approach
- Source: SAMHSA, Trauma-Informed Approaches and Programs, https://www.samhsa.gov/mental-health/trauma-violence/trauma-informed-approaches-programs . VERIFIED from the page. Definition: a trauma-informed "program, organization, or system... realizes the widespread impact of trauma and understands potential paths for recovery; recognizes the signs and symptoms of trauma in clients, families, staff, and others involved with the system; and responds by fully integrating knowledge about trauma into policies, procedures, and practices, while seeking to actively resist retraumatization."
- The principles as that page lists them (five): "Safety: participants and staff feel physically and psychologically safe." "Peer support: peer support and mutual self-help are key as vehicles for establishing safety and hope, building trust, enhancing collaboration..." "Trustworthiness and Transparency: Organizational decisions are conducted with the goal of building and maintaining trust with participants and staff." "Collaboration and Mutuality: importance is placed on partnering and leveling power differences between staff and service participants." "Empowerment, Voice and Choice: organizations foster a belief in the primacy of the people who are served to heal and promote recovery from trauma."
- **The sixth principle** ("Cultural, Historical, and Gender Issues") is **NOT VERIFIED from SAMHSA**: it is not on the page above, and I could not open SAMHSA's 2014 concept paper (HTTP 403). Search summaries and a faith-community toolkit name it. I use it below as "named in secondary sources".

### 1e. Faith-community and pastoral care
- **Pastoral confidentiality norm.** The American Association of Pastoral Counselors code, quoted in Duane R. Bidwell, "Confidences", *Sightings* (University of Chicago Divinity School), 2004: https://divinity.uchicago.edu/sightings/articles/confidences-duane-r-bidwell . VERIFIED: "We do not disclose client confidences to anyone, except: as mandated by law; to prevent a clear and immediate danger to someone; in the course of civil, criminal or disciplinary action arising from the counseling where the pastoral counselor is a defendant; for purposes of supervision or consultation..." And Bidwell: "Clergy and mental-health practitioners have an ethical (and often legal) responsibility to break confidentiality when children or the elderly are being abused or when people are a danger to themselves or others." This is a counselor code, not a rule for every pastor, and I did not read the AAPC code itself.
- **Trauma-informed faith toolkit.** North Carolina Council of Churches, "Sacred Series Toolkit: Becoming a Trauma-Informed Faith Community": https://ncchurches.org/sacred-series-toolkit-becoming-a-trauma-informed-faith-community/ . VERIFIED: the steps are "Expanded and tailored for faith communities from the “Six Key Principles of a Trauma-Informed Approach” by SAMSA (Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Association)". Under Trustworthiness and Transparency it asks: "Are confidences kept? Does unintentional sharing of information happen, even with good intentions such as the example of a prayer chain, prayer requests?" This is one council's toolkit, not a national standard.
- **NOT VERIFIED:** a U.S. Department of Homeland Security Blue Campaign toolkit with the Center for Faith and Opportunity Initiatives was named in a search summary. I did not open it.
- **Not found:** a national standard for confidentiality for every pastor. Norms vary by tradition and state law.

## 4. Five small additions, cheapest first
Effort figures are my estimates, not measurements. Any change to prompts or checks touches the frozen core and needs a re-run, so I mark those.

1. **Case status, follow-up date and close.** Three fields on a saved case: open, waiting or closed; an optional date; a one-line closing note when closed. Serves NASW 6 (monitoring) and the "closure" function, and gives the pastor the "what is still open" view the next-steps map already implies. About 2 to 3 hours. UI and case files only; no prompt change.
2. **A consent and confidentiality note, in plain words.** A short block on the intake and the case page: what Nury saves, that nothing is sent to the family, that the pastor stays responsible for any duty to report danger. It must be truthful: today anyone who can open the app can open the cases, so the note has to say so until sign-in exists. Serves NASW informed consent and confidentiality, the pastoral confidentiality norm, and SAMHSA trustworthiness. About 1 to 2 hours plus Juan's wording. Not legal advice.
3. **Make the update form a real outcome record.** The "What happened?" form already records a step, a result and a note. Show it as a timeline on the case and add a one-tap "resolved" result. Serves outcome tracking (NASW 9, "evaluation of outcomes"). About 2 hours. UI only.
4. **An access note: when, not who.** Log each time a case is opened or exported, with a time. Without sign-in it cannot say who, and the label must say "when, not who". Serves record keeping and SAMHSA transparency. About 2 hours. It becomes "who" only after sign-in.
5. **Ask what the family wants.** Add a triage question for the family's own request and any strength they named ("what they asked for"). Serves NASW assessment (goals and strengths) and SAMHSA voice and choice. About 1 hour of prompt work, **but it changes the frozen core and needs a re-run**, so it is last.

Not on the list because they are large: sign-in and roles, per-church separation, encryption at rest, a native-speaker review of the Spanish, and a professional review of the sources.

## 5. The short version (deck and Q and A)
**Three lines for the deck:**
1. Nury is informed by case-management and trauma-informed practice: it gathers the facts, makes a plan, leaves every decision to the pastor, and saves a record.
2. It is a drafting aid for a pastor, not a professional case-management system, and no standards body has endorsed it.
3. We know the gaps: case status and follow-up, a consent note, an access log, and sign-in.

**Q: "Is Nury compliant with case-management standards?"**
No, and we do not claim it. Nury is informed by the NASW case-management standards and by trauma-informed practice. Examples we can show: structured triage, a service plan with a next-steps map, the pastor deciding at every gate, a saved record, and private notes that never reach a model. Gaps we can name: no case status or follow-up date, no consent note, no access log, and no sign-in or encryption at rest yet. It is a drafting aid, not a professional system. We read the NASW standards in full and CMSA's public page only: the CMSA standards are behind a membership.

**Q: "Do pastors have a confidentiality standard?"**
Norms exist and vary. One example is the American Association of Pastoral Counselors code, which allows disclosure when the law requires it or to prevent a clear and immediate danger. We found no national standard for every pastor. Nury keeps a pastor's private notes out of every model request and never sends anything to a family. The pastor stays responsible for any duty to report.
