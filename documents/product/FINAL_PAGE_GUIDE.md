# Final page guide: a clear, bank-style page for the family

Written 2026-10-07 by hack-ninja for hack-sensei and hack-artisans. Research, short. The page is what the pastor shows or sends to the family after the five stages. It must read like a clear bank notice: the key facts first, one action per line, no fear.

**Source status words.** VERIFIED = I read the primary page or PDF text this session. SECONDARY = from a search summary or a page I could not open; treat as a lead. Nothing here is a claim that Nury meets a standard.

## 1. Twelve rules

| # | Rule | Source (status) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One main message, in the first section.** Put it in 1 to 3 short sentences at the top, fully visible without scrolling. | CDC Clear Communication Index, items 1 and 2: "The main message must be in the first paragraph or section." https://www.cdc.gov/ccindex/pdf/clear-communication-user-guide.pdf (VERIFIED) |
| 2 | **Summarize what the reader needs in a box at the top**, then stop. Include only the most important information. Give a way to get more. | CCI item 10 (VERIFIED). CFPB put "interest rates, monthly payments, the loan amount, and closing costs" on page one of its redesigned forms; users answered 29 percent more questions correctly. https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/201311_cfpb_factsheet_kbyo_testing.pdf (VERIFIED) |
| 3 | **Start with what to do.** Say what the reader should do, then how, in steps. | CCI items 5 and 14: calls to action, with "specific directions about how to perform the behavior" (VERIFIED). CDC CERC: "Promote Action: Giving people meaningful things to do calms anxiety, helps restore order, and promotes some sense of control." https://www.cdc.gov/cerc/ (SECONDARY: search summary; the manual PDF would not open) |
| 4 | **One action per line, in a numbered or bulleted list.** Break lists of more than seven items into groups. | CCI item 8: "Lists with more than seven items must be broken into sub-lists." (VERIFIED). digital.gov: use "lists for easy scanning." https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/design (VERIFIED) |
| 5 | **Short sections with clear headings.** One idea per chunk. | CCI item 9: "A chunk should be only one idea." (VERIFIED). digital.gov: "heading terms that are clear and descriptive." (VERIFIED) |
| 6 | **Active voice, present tense, "you".** Say who does what. | digital.gov: "Active voice makes it clear who should do what." https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/writing (VERIFIED). CCI item 6 (VERIFIED). |
| 7 | **Use the family's own everyday words. No jargon.** Replace legal and medical terms with common ones, and explain a term once if you must keep it. No slang. | CCI item 7: "words they use every day." (VERIFIED) |
| 8 | **Write for a 6th-grade reader or lower.** People under stress take in less; aim about four grades below the audience's average. Aim at 6th grade, and prefer 5th for the top box. | AHRQ toolkit: "best for materials to be written at the 5th or 6th grade level" (SECONDARY: tool page returned 403). "Four grade levels below" is from CDC CERC (SECONDARY: could not open the manual). |
| 9 | **Few numbers, in common terms, each one explained.** Phone numbers with area code, hours with time zone, no maths for the reader. Delete numbers that do not support the main message. | CCI items 15 to 17 (VERIFIED). Phone format itself is common practice, not from a source. |
| 10 | **Calm, respectful, honest. Say what we know and do not know. No fear language, no promises.** Acknowledge the hardship in one plain sentence. | CERC principles: be right, be credible, express empathy, show respect (SECONDARY). Nury rule: no promise phrases ("we will find you a lawyer") and no predictions. |
| 11 | **Readable design.** Body text 16 px or larger, black on white or paper, at least 4.5 to 1 contrast, short lines, white space, bold only for the few key words. No all capitals, no underline except links. Every phone number is a `tel:` link; buttons are at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels (WCAG AA minimum is 24). | CFPB model forms: "Sufficient white space," "Sufficient contrast... black text on white paper," 10-point body (VERIFIED, https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/h/). digital.gov: "Only emphasize important information," avoid all capitals (VERIFIED). WCAG 4.5 to 1 contrast: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html (SECONDARY). Target size AA 24 by 24 CSS px: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html (VERIFIED); the 44 px figure is the enhanced level (SECONDARY); `tel:` is standard HTML practice (not sourced). |
| 12 | **The Spanish is its own plain text, same structure, not word for word.** Same headings, same order, same list lengths. Have a native speaker read it. | CMS: translate, then have a different person translate back; "some graphics, words, or phrases that are perfectly acceptable in English are unacceptable or offensive in other languages." https://www.cms.gov/Outreach-and-Education/MLN/WBT/MLN2059239-Language-Access-Plans/lap/lesson03/11-Translation-Best-Practices/index.html (VERIFIED). Concept equivalence over literal equivalence, WHO (SECONDARY). |

**Two limits worth saying.** CDC says plain language is law for federal materials and that its Index "is not a substitute for the Federal Plain Language Guidelines." And CDC warns that readability formulas "don't consider audience, purpose, or the majority of communication characteristics that contribute to clarity," so a good score does not prove a family understood. Test with a person (section 5).

## 2. Page skeleton

Top to bottom. Phone first. The same skeleton in English (for the pastor) and Spanish (for the family).

1. **Headline sentence** (the main message, 1 to 2 short sentences, largest type). Example shape: "Call a lawyer tonight. These numbers can help."
2. **Key facts box** (the bank-style box; four lines at most):
   - **Who:** the person this is about (first name only, or "your family").
   - **What happened:** one line, plain.
   - **What to do now:** the one most important action, with a call button.
   - **Who sent this:** "From Pastor [name], [church]. Prepared with Nury, an AI assistant."
3. **What is happening.** Three to five short sentences. Facts only, from the case. Say what is not known yet. No advice, no prediction.
4. **What to do next.** Numbered, one action per line. Three groups: "Tonight", "This week", "Gather these papers". No more than seven lines per group. Do not say "do not sign anything" unless the vetted source says it; the source is cited.
5. **Spiritual support.** The pastor's short message (under 120 words) and, only when switched on, a verse from the verified list. The pastor's words come first; the family decides if they want it.
6. **Resources.** Resource cards (section 3). National hotlines first, then the church's own contacts, then official lists.
7. **Footer.** "This is legal information, not legal advice. Nury is an AI assistant, not a pastor, lawyer or counselor. A lawyer can tell you what applies to your case." Date prepared. Language.

## 3. Resource card spec

One card per resource. Same fields, same order, every time.

| Order | Field | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Name** | The organization's own name, as on its page. |
| 2 | **Source label** (a chip, always shown) | "Your church's list" for contacts the pastor vetted. "Official list: [publisher], checked [date]" for an official list. Never "recommended". Under any list: "Listed does not mean recommended." |
| 3 | **What they help with** | One line, plain words, in the page's language. |
| 4 | **Call** (large button) | The number as a `tel:` link, with area code, as "(303) 555-0100". Add "Text" or "Web" only if the source says so. |
| 5 | **Hours** | As the source states, with time zone. If the source does not say: "Hours: ask when you call." Never guess. |
| 6 | **Languages** | As the source states ("Spanish: yes"). If unknown: "Ask for Spanish when you call." |
| 7 | **Cost** | Only if the source says. Never write "free" without a source line (the DOJ list does not say services are free). Otherwise: "Ask about cost when you call." |
| 8 | **Address or website** | Only from the vetted source. |
| 9 | **Checked** | Date the pastor or the source was last verified. |

Rules: no endorsement words ("best", "trusted", "highly rated"); no contact that is not in a vetted source; a missing field shows its honest fallback, never a blank or a guess.

## 4. Wording that stays out

"Don't worry", "everything will be fine", "we will", "you should" (use "you can" for choices and plain imperatives for steps), statistics about outcomes, any agency or party name beyond the resource's own, "free", "safe", "guaranteed", "recommended", "ICE" and other labels the family did not use.

## 5. Reading-level check we can run

`documents/product/readability_check.py` (standard library only). Run it on the generated text per language:

```
python3 documents/product/readability_check.py page_en.txt --lang en   # target: Flesch-Kincaid grade 8 or lower
python3 documents/product/readability_check.py page_es.txt --lang es   # target: INFLESZ 55 or higher
```

- **English:** Flesch-Kincaid grade and Flesch Reading Ease. Target here: grade 8 or lower for the whole page, and 6 or lower for the key facts box and the headline.
- **Spanish:** Szigriszt-Pazos (INFLESZ scale) and Fernández-Huerta. Spanish has no grade level. Target: INFLESZ 55 or higher ("normal" or easier).
- It also prints words per sentence. Flag any sentence over 20 words.
- Exit code 1 on a miss, so a test can call it.

**What is verified, and what is not.**
- Verified: the script runs, and on three samples it gave the expected direction (a short plain English message scored grade 1.7, a jargon sentence scored grade 26.7, a short Spanish message scored INFLESZ 74.6 to 76.9).
- Not verified: the syllable count is a simple heuristic, so scores are approximate, about a grade off. The formulas are written as commonly published; I did not read their primary papers this session. Spanish thresholds (INFLESZ 55) come from the commonly published scale and are not checked against a primary source here.
- A formula is a tripwire, not proof. CDC says formulas "are not sufficient for ensuring that communication is clear and effective." To check a real page: ask two people outside the team to read the key facts box and say back (teach-back) what to do tonight. The Index itself (20 items, 90 or higher to pass) can be scored by hand; the score sheet is at https://www.cdc.gov/ccindex/pdf/full-index-score-sheet.pdf (named in search results; I did not open it).
