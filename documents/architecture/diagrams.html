System context

The pastor talks to the Nury app on a phone. Between the engine and Gloo sits the privacy layer, so Gloo sees tokens, not names. Nury reads the vetted sources and the pastor's own church network, and the app saves cases and the audit log on the server that runs it, uploading them to no one. There is no sign-in, no per-church separation and no encryption at rest yet. Nury has no web access and no way to send anything to the family. The pastor shares text by hand.

Layers and ownership

The app and the eval harness both call the engine. The engine runs a stage, the gates, chaining and the audit log. The safety floor lives in code, not in data. Skills, privacy, the network and the case file are modules around it. A playbook is data. It can add checks and words, and so can a skill. Neither can remove the floor.

Anatomy of a playbook

A new crisis is a new folder. The loader checks the folder before the engine uses it, and a playbook whose sources are not all approved does not run. Paths are plain field matches on the triage text. Some sources fill in at run time, such as the church network. Detention and hospital are live. Sudden death and house fire are cards only.

One run, five stages

Each stage ends at a gate: Approve, Edit, or Stop. Later stages read the approved or edited text, so a pastor's edit carries forward. A run ends in one of four outcomes. Nothing is sent. The pastor shares the package, or saves it as a case.

Correction loop, one stage

The engine drafts through the privacy layer, checks, and either shows a safe draft or rejects it with a reason category. A rejected draft goes to the audit log and the eval record, never to the pastor. The engine regenerates with the reasons, not the draft. Three attempts in total, then it escalates and shows no draft. The fault-injection point is how the demo and the evals force a rejection.

Privacy layer

Nury sends no direct identifiers to a model. The pastor confirms which names to protect. Names, phones, emails, street addresses, dates and ID numbers become tokens before the request leaves, and become real again when the reply returns. The map is kept by the app and never sent to the model. A leak test captures the request body and finds none of the canary values. Built and measured live on three scenarios: all passed, about 4 to 5 percent more input tokens. It removes direct identifiers only.

Vetted sources, network, official list

Contacts come from three places: the playbook's approved sources, the DOJ official list as Juan approved it, and the pastor's own church network. All three are built, tested offline, and checked on live runs. Church contacts are listed first, labeled as the church's own. Nothing is ranked or called free.

Skills: voice and grounding

A skill is a small versioned text module that a stage names in stages.json. The loader validates it and refuses one that tries to override the floor. The text joins the prompt with no extra model call, and the audit log records which skills ran. They can be switched off to compare before and after. The before and after run is scripted and waits for credits.

Case file and next-steps map

After the pastor approves a package, Nury can save it as a folder of linked pages, built without a model call from approved text only. The next-steps map shows steps and questions, never outcomes. A saved case can be revised: the earlier version stays untouched and the new one sits beside it. Revision ran live: version 1 stayed identical byte for byte.

Eval harness and red-team panel

Each scenario runs through an adapter that drives the real engine with privacy on. Deterministic judges score the trajectory with no AI. Jev, the Jev decision API from TypeSafe (a third-party service), adds typed verdicts at eval time. A panel of three non-Claude reviewers reads what the pastor saw and quotes anything unsafe; any finding or disagreement goes to a human. Attacker intakes try to break the rules. The panel and the attacker runs are built; their live runs are pending.
